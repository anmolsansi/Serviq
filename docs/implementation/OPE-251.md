# OPE-251 — What We Decided for the Serviq Demo, and Why

**Ticket:** OPE-251 — V1.0.01 — Freeze the V1 public demo company and synthetic tool domain  
**GitHub issue:** #2  
**Branch:** `ope-251-demo-domain-freeze`  
**Status:** In Progress  
**Audience:** Anyone who wants to understand this work, including readers with little or no software-engineering background.

---

## 1. What is this ticket about?

Serviq is being built as an AI customer-support and customer-operations platform. In the future, a real company could connect its help documents, customer data, order systems, payment systems, and support team to Serviq.

Before we can build that full platform, we need one realistic example that proves the idea works from beginning to end.

Think of it like building a driving simulator. We are not trying to recreate every road in the world on day one. We first need one believable city with roads, traffic lights, cars, and rules so that we can prove the simulator actually works.

For Serviq, that means choosing:

1. a realistic company/customer-support scenario;
2. realistic public documentation the AI can read;
3. realistic private business data such as orders, deliveries, payments, and refunds;
4. a few actions the AI is allowed to perform;
5. clear safety rules around those actions.

OPE-251 freezes those decisions so future engineers do not make different assumptions.

---

## 2. The simple version of the decision

We decided to use a **DoorDash-style customer-support experience** as the main business scenario and **Stripe as a separate reference payment-provider domain**.

That means the demo can handle questions such as:

- "Where is my order?"
- "My order says delivered, but I did not receive it."
- "An item is missing. What can I do?"
- "Am I eligible for a refund?"
- "Please refund the missing item."

DoorDash gives us a very understandable delivery/support scenario. Stripe gives us a clear payment/refund scenario.

Important: **we are not claiming that DoorDash uses Stripe.** In our demo, they are two separate reference systems connected through Serviq. The goal is to show that Serviq can work across multiple business systems instead of being hard-coded to one database or one vendor.

---

## 3. What is real and what is fake in the demo?

This distinction is extremely important.

### Real

We may reference publicly available DoorDash support information and publicly available Stripe documentation where access and usage are permitted.

This public information teaches Serviq general rules and explanations, such as what a delivery status means or how refund behavior works.

### Synthetic

All private operational data is created by us for the demo.

For example:

```text
Customer: Demo Customer 102
Order: DEMO-ORDER-7311
Restaurant: Demo Kitchen
Delivery status: Delivered
Missing item: Fries
Payment: DEMO-PAY-9921
Refundable amount: $4.50
```

None of this belongs to a real DoorDash customer or a real Stripe account.

We use synthetic data because real customer data would create privacy, security, legal, and access problems and is completely unnecessary for proving the product.

---

## 4. Why combine DoorDash and Stripe instead of using only Stripe?

Originally, OPE-251 selected Stripe by itself.

Stripe was a safe and technically clean choice because payment states and refunds are easy to explain. However, a Stripe-only demo mostly looks like a payment-support product.

Serviq is supposed to be broader than that. It is meant to sit between a customer and a business and help resolve the whole problem.

A delivery example gives us more of the real customer journey:

```text
Customer places order
        ↓
Order is prepared
        ↓
Delivery is assigned
        ↓
Order is delivered
        ↓
Something goes wrong
        ↓
Customer asks Serviq for help
        ↓
Serviq reads support rules
        ↓
Serviq checks the customer's synthetic order
        ↓
Serviq decides what can be done
        ↓
Serviq checks the payment/refund side
        ↓
Serviq either solves the issue or sends it to a human
```

This is a much better demonstration of a customer-operations platform.

## 5. What systems are we pretending exist?

For the demo, we need private business records that look like the records a real company might have.

We therefore froze these synthetic record types:

### `demo_customers`

Represents the customer.

A simple way to think about this is the customer's profile card inside the demo.

It can contain a fake customer ID and other safe demo information needed to prove that Serviq is looking at the correct customer's data.

### `demo_orders`

Represents an order.

It can contain things such as:

- demo order number;
- order total;
- order status;
- created time;
- customer relationship.

### `demo_order_items`

Represents the individual things inside the order.

This matters because "my order is missing" and "one item is missing" are different problems.

### `demo_deliveries`

Represents the delivery itself.

For example:

```text
assigned → picked_up → on_the_way → delivered
```

This gives Serviq a trustworthy source for questions such as "Where is my order?"

### `demo_order_events`

Stores a timeline of what happened.

For example:

```text
7:01 PM — order placed
7:10 PM — restaurant confirmed
7:32 PM — driver picked up
7:55 PM — order delivered
```

This helps Serviq explain the current state rather than just returning one word.

### `demo_payments`

Represents the payment associated with the synthetic order.

It does **not** contain a real card number and does not connect to a real customer's bank account.

### `demo_refund_rules`

Represents the business rules Serviq must follow before creating a refund.

For example:

```text
Missing item worth less than $20 → customer confirmation required
Refund above $50 → human approval required
Already fully refunded → do not refund again
```

This is important because the AI should never invent business rules.

### `demo_refunds`

Stores the fake refund after Serviq is allowed to perform one.

This record proves that Serviq can safely perform a state-changing action without moving real money.

### `demo_support_cases`

Represents a support case when the AI cannot or should not finish the problem alone.

This allows a human support agent to continue from where the AI stopped.

## 6. What are the three important tools?

In Serviq, a **tool** is simply a safe function the AI is allowed to ask the backend to run.

A useful analogy is a hotel employee. The employee may be able to look up your reservation, check the cancellation rules, and cancel the booking. Those are three different permissions.

Serviq works the same way.

### Tool 1 — `demo.get_delivery_order_status`

This is a **read-only** tool.

Its job is to look at the synthetic order and delivery records and tell Serviq what is actually happening.

Example:

```text
Customer: Where is my order?

AI does NOT guess.
AI asks the tool.

Tool returns:
Order DEMO-1234
Status: on_the_way
Driver picked up: 7:32 PM
Estimated arrival: 8:05 PM
```

Why we built it this way:

- structured facts should come from data, not AI memory;
- it reduces hallucinations;
- it is faster and cheaper than asking a large model to reason about something the database already knows.

### Tool 2 — `demo.check_order_resolution_eligibility`

This is also read-only.

Its job is to answer: **"What solutions are allowed for this problem?"**

For a missing item, it could consider:

- which item is missing;
- the price of that item;
- order status;
- whether a refund already happened;
- current refund rules;
- whether customer confirmation is required;
- whether a human manager must approve it.

The important design principle is that the AI does not get to simply say, "Sure, I will refund you."

The rules engine decides what is allowed.

### Tool 3 — `demo.create_refund`

This is a **protected mutation**.

"Mutation" is a technical word that means the system changes stored data.

Looking up a refund is a read. Creating a refund is a mutation.

Because mutations have consequences, Serviq checks several things first:

```text
Correct customer?
        ↓
Correct order/payment?
        ↓
Eligible under the rules?
        ↓
Customer confirmation required?
        ↓
Human approval required?
        ↓
Has this exact refund already been submitted?
        ↓
Only then create the synthetic refund
```

The V1 demo does not send money through Stripe. It only creates a safe synthetic refund record.

This lets us demonstrate the engineering logic without risking real financial activity.

## 7. What does the AI actually do in this flow?

The AI is not the database and it is not the bank.

Its job is closer to an intelligent coordinator.

Example:

```text
Customer:
"The fries were missing. Can you refund them?"

1. Serviq understands the request.
2. Serviq retrieves the relevant support knowledge.
3. Serviq looks up the customer's synthetic order.
4. Serviq confirms that fries were part of the order.
5. Serviq checks the resolution/refund rules.
6. Serviq asks for confirmation if required.
7. Serviq asks for human approval if policy requires it.
8. Serviq calls the protected refund tool.
9. Serviq stores an audit trail of what happened.
10. Serviq tells the customer the result.
```

This architecture is safer than allowing an LLM to directly edit databases or call payment APIs however it wants.

## 8. How public documentation is handled

We do **not** want to copy an entire company's help center into this GitHub repository.

Instead, the project stores a controlled source list, called a **source manifest**.

Think of it as a reading list for the AI:

```text
Topic: missing items
Source: approved public URL
Last checked: date
Enabled: yes/no
```

The current combined policy is named:

```text
doordash-stripe-allowlist-v1
```

"Allowlist" means Serviq only reads sources that have been explicitly approved. It does not receive permission to wander around a website downloading everything it can find.

If automated access is not allowed for a source, we do not bypass that restriction. The architecture must either use an allowed source, manually supplied permitted material, or another reference source.

## 10. What does this improve?

This ticket improves the project in several ways.

### It removes ambiguity

Future tickets no longer have to ask, "What kind of customer/order/payment data should we build?"

### It creates one end-to-end story

A recruiter, contributor, client, or reviewer can follow one believable support journey rather than disconnected AI features.

### It proves multiple system integration

Serviq must combine public knowledge, private order data, delivery state, payment state, business rules, actions, and human escalation.

### It makes AI safer

The AI understands language, but deterministic tools and policies control facts and actions.

### It keeps the public demo safe

No real private customer data is used and no real payment is performed.

## 11. What we intentionally did NOT build in this ticket

OPE-251 is primarily a product and architecture decision ticket.

We did not yet:

- build the order database;
- build the delivery database;
- connect to a real DoorDash account;
- connect to a real Stripe account;
- scrape an entire help center;
- implement the three tools;
- create real refunds;
- build the customer chat screen.

Those belong to later implementation tickets.

The purpose here is to make those future tickets precise.

## 12. How will we know this decision works?

The finished demo should eventually prove scenarios such as:

1. customer asks for order status;
2. Serviq returns a verified synthetic delivery status;
3. customer reports a missing item;
4. Serviq explains the relevant support policy using approved knowledge;
5. Serviq checks what resolutions are allowed;
6. customer requests a refund;
7. Serviq requests confirmation;
8. human approval occurs when required;
9. Serviq creates one synthetic refund and does not duplicate it;
10. the complete decision trail is visible to support staff and in audit history.

## 13. Plain-English glossary

**AI / LLM:** The language model that understands and generates natural-language responses.

**RAG:** A method where the AI first looks up relevant documents and then answers using those documents instead of relying only on memory.

**Tool:** A controlled backend function the AI can request, such as looking up an order.

**Read-only:** Can inspect data but cannot change it.

**Mutation:** An operation that changes stored data.

**Synthetic data:** Fake but realistic data created specifically for testing and demonstrations.

**Policy engine:** The part of Serviq that decides whether an action is allowed, denied, needs customer confirmation, or needs human approval.

**Idempotency:** A safety mechanism that prevents the same action from accidentally happening twice when the same request is repeated.

**Audit trail:** A history explaining who or what did something, when it happened, and why.

**Source manifest:** The approved list of documents Serviq may use as knowledge sources.
